===== R3E2 (outcome FAILED, candidates tried 7)
The path cannot be completed, because the target statement 'TP_rho, E_rho, O_rho hold for every rho' is false.

What works (proved in /tmp/claude-0/qu/explore3/R3E2/proof_TPE.txt):
- TP_rho holds for every rho, by cyclic unimodality (T11 Lemma 7) plus e>=0 on the lower half.
- E_rho holds for every rho, even without the lower bound K>=2mu*_inf. It is the automatic half: alternating sums of a nonnegative unimodal sequence whose total is a class sum >=0.
- O_rho is equivalent to one inequality: r(N*(rho)+5) > sigma+1-2mu*_inf(k,tau). Equivalently N*(rho) >= floor((sigma+1-2mu*_inf)/r)-3, and the slack is always 1 or 3 when O holds. So Theorem R reduces to that single inequality (Corollary 5).

Where it breaks: O_rho is false.
- Explicit fit-box counterexample: r=57, rho=(1^37,6,7,9,15,18,20,22,23,25,26,27,28,30,33,37), k=52, sigma=311. P_2(rho) is not unimodal, so N*=1 and K_1=141. mu*_inf=-16, so 2mu*_inf+3r=139<=141 and O fails. With 36 ones it holds (mu*_inf=14).
- More cases with minimal padding m (number of ones): m=64 (r=50), m=73 (r=31), m=58 (r=28), m=98 (r=21).
- 44 of 137 random padded rho' (k=140) fail O.
- Without residue-1 parts, O held in 1500 of 1500 cases; with few ones, in 2893 of 2893.

Mechanism (proved): ones change only k, hence f, hence mu*_inf, which drops toward mu_neg >= -r. Meanwhile N*(rho) and mu*(rho) stay fixed. Once binom(k,2)>=max tau, O becomes the strong certificate O+, which is known to fail (R2E2: 25,737 rho').

The failure is in the certificate, not in S2. Lifts of the counterexample have U=[1,40]u{42}, [1,55]u{57}, [1,144]u{146}, [1,300]u{302}, and ND_a(K_1) holds for each. Lifting strictly improves ND, and the residue level loses this.

Consequence: any certificate of this kind fails on rho padded with ones. That covers residue-level ND together with any lower bound on mu*(a) that is uniform over all lifts. The k-dependence through mu*_inf runs the wrong way.

===== R3E4: refute Conjecture S2 globally at large r (r >= 1000) (outcome NONEXISTENCE_EVIDENCE, candidates tried 316601)
I found no counterexample to S2. Every instance I evaluated (316,601 evaluations, all with r >= 1000, up to r = 50000) has beta - N* in {0, 2}. A global S2 failure needs beta - N* >= 4.

Measures I used:
- gap = umin - vmin, the continuous crossing positions of the two threshold sequences T_{n+1} and T_{b-1}+y_b, in index units. A failure needs gap >= 4. In well-resolved instances the gap stays at 2.0-2.34. The baseline of 2 is structural.
- The failure margin min_C log((T_{N*+3}+y_{N*+4})/|A_C|), which must be >= 0. The best value is -1.135 levels, about -6.8 nats.
- Exact checks: the two best new near-misses were verified by brute force and by the exact pair criterion (verify_nearmiss.py).

Why it fails (heuristic, quantitative, not a proof):
- In level units with decay kappa per r-step, pair C has beta_C ~ 2*floor(u_C)+1 and n*(C) ~ 2*floor(u_C - delta_C)+1. Here u_C = -ln|tau(t_C)|/kappa - C/(2r), and delta_C ~ ln((1-e^-kappa)/(1-e^{-kappa(r-C)/r}))/kappa is large only for small-gap pairs, C near r.
- A global failure needs min_C u_C >= min_C(u_C - delta_C) + 1.
- Antisymmetry forces tau to vanish at the anti-centre class C = r. So |tau| grows linearly in g' = r-C there, and this cancels the delta advantage of the small-gap pairs to first order.
- With a sinusoidal tau the excess is only about 0.07 levels at kappa ~ 5-6. This matches the data (mean 0.065 vs 0.068).
- Escaping needs either kappa < ~1, or a tau concentrated within about r e^{-kappa}/kappa classes of the anti-centre.

Neither is reachable:
- kappa: the binomial-tail (lifted) regime gives kappa >~ pi*e*(1-2/k). The Gaussian regime gives kappa >= 2*pi. The smallest resolved kappa found by a dedicated minimisation (hc4) and in the surveys was about 3.9.
- Concentrated tau: this needs many comparable harmonics. That happens only with small effective residues (s or r-s, with s << r). These make |tau| tiny, push the crossing to the extreme tail, and make kappa about e*r/s, which is huge.
- The designed family built on exactly this idea (two residues near r/2 for a triangle-wave Gamma, small-width parts, lifted +-1 parts; 216 instances) gives beta = N* in every case, with margin -50.

Closest near-misses:
- r=15167, k=111 (the r2 instance): margin -1.135 levels.
- r=5000, k=150, a_i in [1394,1493]: margin -1.19 levels; exact U contains 9,10,11 and is not unimodal at b = 12..16 (b = 1..8 not checked).
- r=8000, a=(12871,12875,12921,20863,20986): largest resolved excess, 0.30 index units; exact U contains 6,7,8 and is not unimodal at b = 9..13 (b = 1..5 not checked).
A failure needs about +1 level of margin, i.e. excess >= 2.

Limits:
- The search was in floating point. Ties, if any, would need exact rechecks, but no instance came within a level of failing.
- Hill-climbs at r >= 10000 were slow, 1.6k-5k evaluations per run.
- I did not search r > 50000 exhaustively. The model says larger r only helps pair-local failures (r >~ kappa e^kappa), not the global inequality.
- Coarsely sampled instances (D/r small) give gap values up to 3.69, but these are interpolation artifacts; in all of them beta = N*.
