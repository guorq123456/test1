        If it is excluded (exactly 4 or 5 middle residues, 20 <= k <= 23), then |A_inf| <= 5 (Lemma 5b), and C3 with
        Proposition 8 and Lemma 1 gives S2.
r = 6.  k >= 141: Lemma 13.  54 <= k <= 140: C1-6.  k <= 53: C4 with [R], except the excluded classes (exactly 4 or 5
        middle residues, 20 <= k <= 53), which are covered by C3 (|A_inf| <= 11, Lemma 5c).                         QED

==========================================================================================
7. REMARKS / WHAT FAILED ALONG THE WAY (see candidates.txt)
==========================================================================================
* The residue-level route alone does not give an asymptotic regime.  For r = 4, rho' = (2^{n2}, 3^{n3}) with the
  k -> infinity value of mu*_inf (residue-1 parts taken as size 1), the O condition fails for 179 of 2,998 (n2,n3) with
  n2 + n3 <= 118 in the fit box (scan4_strong.py), all with n2 >= 41.  This is why the large-count regime uses the class-pair
  inequality (L') with the coefficient bounds of Section 3, and not TP/E/O.
* (L') is far from tight in the fit box.  At n*, x_{n*+1} <= 6 in all 2,826 sampled r = 4 instances (lp_stats2.py), and the smallest
  observed ratio (y_{n+1}-y_{n+2})/y_{n+4} is above 3*10^5 for r = 4, 5, 6 (lp_check_r.py).  The proof needs exact
  computation only because the bounds of Lemma 7 are lossy near the binomial inflection point.
* The pair-local form of S2 is false at r = 8595 ([S] Section 9).  Wherever the proof goes through Lemma 1 (all of r = 4;
  r = 5 with k >= 24; r = 6 with k >= 54; and the excluded classes of C3), it proves the pair-local inequality
  beta_C <= n*(C)+2 for every pair.  For the remaining small-k cases (r = 5, k <= 23; r = 6, k <= 53) the proof uses the
  residue certificate [R] + C4.  There (L') at n* is only observed numerically (0 failures in lp_check_r.py), not proved.
* Fit box.  The final proof evaluates exact numbers only for k <= 140, and only for single-value products
  (2^p), (3^s), for k < 60 for B_{p,s}, and inside the box otherwise.  Earlier exploratory runs (analytic_cert.py up to
  k = 700, analytic_cert2.py up to 400, analytic_cert6.py up to 600, hand_checks.py) evaluated generic binomial and trinomial
  coefficients C(k,x), B_{p,s}(x) for larger k as bounds.  These are not P or unimodality data of any instance, but they
  coincide with A-coefficients of instances like (2^k).  The final proof does not depend on them, because the hand
  Lemmas 12 and 13 now start at k = 141.
* Not attempted: r >= 7.  For r = 7 the same scheme should work: the residue certificate C4 is fast (6.6 s for 4.4M
  multisets at r = 6), and C1 needs a V bound with M_1 = max|[rho]_zeta| = 2.247.
