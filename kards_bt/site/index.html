    怎么算的

    每场比赛（一个 Bo3/Bo5 系列赛算一场）给出“谁赢了谁”。BT 模型假设：

    P(i 胜 j) = 1 / (1 + e−(βi − βj))，　BT 分 = 1500 + β × 400 / ln 10
    • 随时间变化：每站比赛结束后和每月 1 日，用截至当时的全部对局重新拟合；距今 d 天的对局权重为 0.5d/240。
    • 先验：β ~ N(0, 0.4²)，相当于每人先带一点“平均水平”的虚拟战绩，只打过两三场的人不会被冲到榜首。
    • 区间：± 是拉普拉斯近似给出的 1 个标准误。场次越少，区间越宽。
    • 参数怎么定的：用每站赛前的分数预测这一站的胜负（清洗后 3,432 场），半衰期 240 天、先验 0.4 的 log loss 是 0.687（抛硬币是 0.693；预测准确率约 53%）。官方赛事的参赛者水平接近，胜负本来就难预测，分差小于 100 分的对局基本是五五开。按赛事级别加权、预选降权、按 Bo3/Bo5 和 ban 位区分赛制，都检验过，样本外预测没有变好，所以没有采用。
    • 没打的比赛不算：轮空、平台判负、Battlefy 只有一方签到后 10 分钟的自动判负、选手被标记 DQ 之后录入（或在 DQ 时被改写）的结果、start.gg 上只记胜负没有比分的缺席判负、管理员几秒内批量清掉的缺席选手对局，整场作废，对手的“胜场”也一起去掉。DQ 之前真打的比赛保留；瑞士轮和循环赛不按负场数判断；只要双方都在场、比分正常，录入再快也算真实对局。
    • 排名分：榜单按 BT 分 − 1 个标准误排序。停赛后旧比赛的权重越来越小，标准误随之变大，排名分自然下降；BT 分本身仍是对实力的最佳估计。检验过“久未出赛直接扣分”：久未出赛的选手回来后反而赢得比模型预期多（停赛 120–240 天的实际胜率高出 6.8 个百分点），扣分只会让预测变差，所以只在排名上体现“不确定”，不改分数。
    • 新人起点：第一次打官方赛的选手实际胜率约 42%，模型若按平均水平算会给到约 49%，被高估了。而且新人比平均弱多少随时代变化（2022 年约 46 分，2023–2025 年约 90 分，2026 年约 49 分）。所以每站赛前，用过去一年新人的实际表现估计这个差距，新人从这个位置起步（相当于 2 场虚拟的平局，按 240 天半衰期淡出）。2024 年后的检验里预测略有改善（log loss −0.0055），但还在误差范围内，属于有依据、待更多数据确认的修正。
    • 小样本和“赢家诅咒”：分数几乎全来自预选的选手，到了正赛平均比预测少赢约 4 个百分点。能进正赛的人是被“预选打得好”筛出来的，其中一部分是手感和运气，进了正赛自然回落。降低预选权重检验过，修不好这一点，预测还变差，所以没有采用；更可靠的是看排名分，场次少的人标准误大，排名分会更保守。
    • 天梯直邀：OCC 8 强里没打资格赛的直邀选手，记上当月资格赛晋级者的中位战绩（直邀和资格赛胜出一样难），按 120 天半衰期淡出。
    • 人工修正：data/aliases.csv 合并同一人的不同账号，data/overrides.csv 把代打的结果记到实际上场的人名下，data/manual_matches.csv 补录只在官方新闻里公布的总决赛。
    • 同一人跨平台：按平台账号 + 游戏内 ID 合并，可在 data/aliases.csv 里手动补充。

    How it works

    Each match (a Bo3/Bo5 series counts as one) tells us who beat whom. The Bradley–Terry model assumes:

    P(i beats j) = 1 / (1 + e−(βi − βj)),　rating = 1500 + β × 400 / ln 10
    • Changes over time: after every event and on the 1st of each month, the model is refit on all matches so far; a match played d days earlier has weight 0.5d/240.
    • Prior: β ~ N(0, 0.4²), like giving everyone a few virtual "average" results, so a player with two or three wins cannot jump to the top.
    • Uncertainty: ± is one standard error from the Laplace approximation. Fewer matches, wider band.
    • How the settings were chosen: each event is predicted from ratings computed before it started (3,432 matches after cleaning). With a 240-day half-life and prior 0.4 the log loss is 0.687 (a coin flip is 0.693; about 53% of winners predicted). Official fields are close in strength, so results are hard to predict: matches between players less than 100 points apart are close to 50/50. Weighting events by tier, down-weighting qualifiers and treating Bo3/Bo5/ban formats differently were all tested; none improved out-of-sample predictions, so neither is used.
    • Unplayed matches do not count: byes, platform forfeits, Battlefy's automatic win 10 minutes after only one player checked in, results entered after (or rewritten at) a player's DQ, start.gg results with no score (the no-show convention), and no-show losses an admin cleared in bulk within seconds are voided, and so is the opponent's "win". Real matches played before a DQ are kept; Swiss and round-robin records are never judged by loss count; and a normally scored match between two present players counts however quickly it was reported.
    • Rank score: the leaderboard is sorted by rating − 1 standard error. Once a player stops competing their old results lose weight, their uncertainty grows and the rank score falls; the rating itself remains the best estimate of strength. A direct penalty for inactivity was tested: players returning from a break actually win more than the model expects (6.8 points above prediction after a 120–240-day break), so a penalty would make predictions worse. Inactivity therefore shows up only as uncertainty in the ranking, not in the rating.
    • Newcomers: first-time official players win about 42% of matches, where an average starting point would predict about 49%. How far below average newcomers are changes with the era (about 46 points in 2022, about 90 in 2023–2025, about 49 in 2026), so before every event the gap is estimated from the past year's newcomers and debutants start there (the equivalent of two virtual draws, fading with the 240-day half-life). On post-2024 data it improves predictions slightly (log loss −0.0055), within the margin of error: a well-founded correction awaiting more data.
    • Small samples and the winner's curse: players whose rating comes almost entirely from qualifiers win about 4 points less than predicted once they reach a main stage. Players reach the main stage by doing well in qualifiers, partly through form and luck, so as a group they fall back. Down-weighting qualifier results was tested; it does not fix this and makes predictions worse, so it is not used. The rank score is the safer guide: few matches mean a wide standard error and a more cautious rank score.
    • Ladder invites: OCC Top 8 players invited from the in-game ladder skip the qualifier, so each is credited with that month's median qualifier-advancer record (an invite is as hard to earn as a qualifier win), fading with a 120-day half-life.
    • Manual corrections: data/aliases.csv merges one person's accounts, data/overrides.csv credits a substitute's results to the person who actually played, and data/manual_matches.csv adds grand finals published only in official news.
    • One person, several platforms: accounts are merged by platform account and in-game ID, with manual additions in data/aliases.csv.
